## 1. Register a Finnish osakeyhtiö. Not Delaware.

**This is now on the critical path**, because the largest money available to us
requires a Finnish company with a Business ID.

Reasons, in order:

1. **Business Finland's Sprint Grant needs it** — up to €100k, non-dilutive.
2. **Our customers expect to contract with a company**, not with a person. It is
   also the cleanest answer to "would you trust a product built by lukio
   students": they are contracting with an Oy that has a DPA, not with a
   teenager.
3. **The GDPR story only works from the EU.** Our whole security position is EU
   data residency. "We're a Delaware company" makes that conversation harder
   with exactly the reviewers currently blocking three pilots.
4. Cheap — a few hundred euros, no minimum share capital.

**Get vesting into the founder agreement at the same time.** Four years with a
one-year cliff is standard; three is defensible but investors usually reset it.
Do it while it is abstract and nobody has a number in their head. Ask for
double-trigger acceleration on a change of control — free to request at
founding, expensive to add later.

### Why people incorporate in Delaware, and why it is not us

**It is not a tax play.** US federal corporate tax is 21% plus state tax;
Finland is 20%. Delaware lowers nothing if you do not operate there, and Finland
would still tax us since management and control sit here.

The real reason is access to US capital:

- **PFIC rules** make holding shares in a foreign corporation punitive for US
  taxpayers. This is a hard blocker for US angels investing in an Oy.
- **Standardised paperwork** — YC SAFEs, NVCA docs. Rounds close in days.
- **Delaware case law is predictable**, so investors price no legal uncertainty.
- **YC and most US accelerators require it.**

All investor-side. If we are not raising from US funds we would be paying for a
structure whose only benefit we do not use — plus US filings, franchise tax, a
registered agent, and a Finnish permanent-establishment question.

If US investors later insist, the standard move is a **flip**: a Delaware
holding company placed above the Oy. Routine, lawyers do it constantly, costs
real money. Cross that bridge at term sheet, not before.

**Why we fit:** it funds early-stage Finnish companies developing innovative
solutions **for international markets**. We are a Finnish team with four signed
LOIs, a Swedish origin story, and Finnish and Swedish prospects. That is close
to the intended profile.

**Tempo funding is closed** — applications shut October 2025 "for the time
being", no 2026 call scheduled. Do not plan around it.
([status](https://www.businessfinland.fi/en/for-finnish-customers/services/funding/tempo-funding))

**Two practical notes:**

- **Reimbursement-based.** We fund our 25% and cash-flow the rest before the
  grant arrives. Budget for the gap.
- **Toivo's father works at Business Finland.** Declare it cleanly in the
  application. It is fine when disclosed and awkward when discovered. Informal
  soundings from his colleagues are not the route — the application is.
