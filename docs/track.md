# Track: AI Agents

**Decided Saturday ~11:50. One track only — the rules changed, we can no longer
tick several.**

---

## The decision

**AI Agents.** Not Outbuild, not Wildcard, not Edtech.

## Why, in one line

It weights us **inversely to our weakness**: the lowest traction weight of any
track, and the highest problem-validation weight. Traction is the thing we have
none of with a day to go. Problem validation is the thing we banked on Friday
night and nobody can take away.

## The arithmetic

Scored honestly. Median = a few demo requests and maybe one LOI. Upside = one
payment plus two pilot agreements.

| | Median | Upside |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **AI Agents** | **~33** | **~46** |
| Wildcard | ~32 | ~50 |
| Outbuild | ~28 | ~44 |
| Edtech B2C | ~22 | ~30 |

| Criterion | Outbuild | **AI Agents** | Wildcard | Edtech |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Traction — our weakest | 18 | **12** | 10 | 16 |
| Problem/validation — our strongest | 8 | **14** | 10 | 12 |
| Business potential | 18 | 16 | 10 | 14 |
| Originality | 6 | 8 | **20** | 8 |

**Wildcard has the higher ceiling and we still didn't take it.** Its 50 rests
almost entirely on one subjective 20-point originality call, and a well-executed
B2B tool reliably loses that to something genuinely strange. Our *story* is
memorable; our *product* is serious. Those score differently on that axis.

**Outbuild puts 36% of the score on the one thing we have none of.**

**Edtech** wants validation "through students or teachers" and we have zero.

## What we're aiming the pitch at

The 14-point band, which is a sentence we can satisfy almost word for word:

> *"Painful, frequent and validated problem where an AI agent is clearly the
> right solution."*

Documentation can't fix it — the role is being invented while they hire for it.
Retrieval can't — it needs the answer to already exist and the newcomer to know
what to ask. A mentor can't — the absence of a spare senior person *is* the
constraint. Therefore an agent. Not because agents are fashionable, because
nothing else is left.

And we found that by asking strangers on a street, not by reasoning about it.

## The traction band is softer here — aim at the right target

- **8 of 12 needs "multiple pilots"**, not payments. Two people agreeing to try
  it on their own Slack gets most of the way, and `/ingest` is what that is for.
- Outbuild's 12 would need a paying customer **and** signed LOIs.

So the afternoon's goal is **one payment and two pilot agreements** — not three
paying customers. That is a genuinely different and much more achievable target.

## The crowding objection is in our favour

Everyone builds "an AI agent" at a hackathon. That is exactly why this rubric
puts **"Generic chatbot or basic AI wrapper" at 0 originality**. The field will
be full of chat wrappers, and the judges are separating people on precisely the
axis where we are strong:

- **Drift detection** — it speaks unprompted, and appears in none of the
  research taxonomies we surveyed
- **Verified grounding** — every citation checked verbatim, unverifiable ones
  dropped, plus sentence expansion so a true fragment can't reverse a meaning
- **An escalation policy that declines to escalate** — the judgment is the
  product

## Judge fit

**Charles Maddock** has been building agentic systems since 2018 and runs a
browser for agent-to-agent internet. He is the one who will ask whether this is
genuinely an agent or a loop with a prompt — and this track is where that
question scores points instead of costing them.

**Wojtek Szkutnik** reads code for a living and scaled a company 3 → 100+ people.
He will open the repo and he has lived the problem.

## What this changes in the pitch

Lead with **why an agent is the only remaining option**, not with customers.

1. The forced conclusion (the three closed doors)
2. Drift detection as proof it isn't a chat wrapper — *"nobody asked it to check"*
3. Grounding as proof it isn't inventing things
4. Traction as **supporting evidence**, not the headline
